\begin{abstract}
Knowledge graph reasoning infers missing facts by propagating query-relevant
evidence across multiple relational hops. Progressive graph neural reasoners
reduce global computation through query-centered frontier expansion, but they
commonly treat edge transformation, frontier construction, and layer-wise state
updating as separate operations. Such decoupling makes it difficult to maintain
coherent evidence when both its representation and propagation support evolve
with reasoning depth. We propose STM-GNN, a Sheaf-Transport Momentum Graph
Neural Network that formulates progressive multi-hop reasoning as
query-conditioned evidence dynamics on an evolving local graph. Each STM
transition establishes a closed evidence loop: query-conditioned sheaf
transport aligns relation-aware evidence, the transported evidence induces the
next propagation frontier, and momentum-coupled dynamics preserve it across
reasoning depth. Edge-wise transport consistency and depth-wise continuity
regularize this transition at complementary scales. By coupling how evidence is
transformed, where it propagates, and how it persists, STM-GNN provides a
unified reasoning process rather than independently combining message
propagation, candidate pruning, and recurrent state updating.

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:introduction}

Knowledge graphs (KGs) organize factual knowledge as relational triples and
support applications such as question answering, recommendation, semantic
search, and decision support \citep{ji2021survey}. Because real-world KGs are
incomplete, knowledge graph completion seeks to infer missing facts from the
observed relational structure. This paper focuses on progressive multi-hop tail
prediction: given a query $q=(s,r_q,?)$, a model propagates relational evidence
outward from the source entity $s$ and ranks all entities as possible answers.
The central challenge is therefore not only to score candidate entities, but
also to preserve query-relevant evidence while both the explored graph region
and the entity states evolve with reasoning depth.

Existing approaches provide complementary views of this challenge. Knowledge
graph embedding models learn entity--relation compatibility functions, but the
multi-hop evidence underlying a prediction is usually implicit
\citep{bordes2013transe,trouillon2016complex,sun2019rotate}. Rule- and path-based
methods expose compositional reasoning chains, although rule coverage,
sequential search, and sparse learning signals can restrict the evidence they
recover \citep{xiong2017deeppath,das2018minerva,yang2017neurallp,
sadeghian2019drum,qu2020rnnlogic}. Recent reinforcement-learning reasoners have
improved path decisions using dynamic query context, neighborhood information,
and relation semantics \citep{shang2024attention,wang2025losa}. GNN reasoners
take a different route by aggregating multiple relational paths within a shared
graph computation, making them well suited to combining distributed evidence
\citep{schlichtkrull2018modeling,vashishth2019composition}.

Progressive and adaptive GNN reasoners make this graph computation dependent on
the query. NBFNet and RED-GNN recursively aggregate relational evidence from the
query source, while DPMPN constructs an input-dependent subgraph
\citep{zhu2021neural,zhang2022knowledge,xu2019dpmpn}. AdaProp learns a
query-aware distribution for retaining promising entities, A*Net learns
priorities for selecting important nodes and edges, and DiffusionE controls
query-relevant message flow through a diffusion formulation
\citep{zhang2022adaprop,zhu2023astarnet,cao2024diffusione}. These advances
substantially improve the relevance and scalability of multi-hop propagation by
replacing indiscriminate global aggregation with an evolving local propagation
support.

Despite these advances, most progressive reasoners adapt where evidence
propagates through scalar attention, sampling probabilities, or priority scores,
while relational transformation and depth-wise state evolution are modeled
through separate representations. Their routing and state updates are therefore
connected only indirectly, without a shared evidence interface that jointly
determines what relational evidence means, where it should propagate, and how
it should persist as the active support evolves.

This observation leads to the \emph{coupled evidence evolution problem}. In
progressive reasoning, edge transport, support evolution, and depth-wise state
evolution should form a closed transition: a change in edge transport should
alter which candidates remain active; the retained evidence should determine
the subsequent state displacement; and the evolved state should condition
transport at the next reasoning depth. Fig.~\ref{fig:motivation} illustrates one
observable consequence of missing this coordination: weakly related evidence
can survive frontier expansion and accumulate along a noisy branch. Such noise
is therefore a symptom of a broader problem involving how evidence is
represented, routed, and retained over depth.

To address this problem, we propose STM-GNN, a Sheaf-Transport Momentum Graph
Neural Network that models progressive reasoning as query-conditioned evidence
dynamics. At each depth, query-conditioned diagonal sheaf restrictions align
relation-aware source evidence with the target-side coordinates of an expanded
edge. The resulting transported evidence is the shared transition variable: its
preview aggregation scores newly reached entities and induces the next active
frontier, while its retained part forms the driving signal for a momentum
recurrence over the hidden states. The evolved hidden and momentum states then
condition transport at the next depth. STM-GNN therefore establishes a closed
reasoning transition in which evidence transformation, support evolution, and
depth-wise memory are coordinated through the same evidence flow rather than
appended as independent processing stages.

The transition is regularized at two complementary scales. Edge-level transport
consistency aligns the source evidence with the target update in a common edge
stalk, while depth-level continuity stabilizes the momentum trajectory across
successive reasoning steps. Together, they preserve relational compatibility
and depth-wise coherence within the same STM transition. The formulation
connects sheaf-based local alignment and knowledge representation
\citep{bodnar2022neural,barbero2022sheaf,gebhart2023knowledge} with second-order
neural state dynamics \citep{sander2021momentum,rusch2022graphcon} in a
query-centered KG reasoning setting.

\section{Related Work}\label{sec:related_work}

\subsection{Knowledge Graph Completion and Multi-hop Reasoning}
\label{subsec:kg_reasoning}

Knowledge graph completion aims to infer missing facts from observed triples.
Embedding-based methods assign continuous representations to entities and
relations and score each candidate triple with a learned compatibility
function. Representative models include translational, bilinear, convolutional,
and complex- or quaternion-valued formulations
\citep{bordes2013transe,trouillon2016complex,dettmers2018convolutional,
sun2019rotate,zhang2019quaternion}. Geometry-aware extensions further adapt the
representation space to relational patterns and hierarchical structures
\citep{wang2023hygge}. These models are effective triple scorers, but the
multi-step evidence supporting a prediction is encoded implicitly in their
parameters rather than represented as an explicit reasoning process.

Multi-hop methods expose more of this process through paths or rules. DeepPath
and MINERVA formulate reasoning as sequential decision making over relational
paths \citep{xiong2017deeppath,das2018minerva}, while Neural LP, DRUM, and
RNNLogic learn differentiable or probabilistic compositions of relations
\citep{yang2017neurallp,sadeghian2019drum,qu2020rnnlogic}. More recent path-based
systems improve action selection with attentive path information or local
semantic context \citep{shang2024attention,wang2025losa}. Although these methods
produce interpretable trajectories or rules, their coverage is bounded by the
paths explored during search or by the learned rule inventory. Graph-based
reasoners instead aggregate distributed evidence from multiple paths within a
shared computation. Unlike triple-scoring methods and single-path reasoners,
STM-GNN operates on a query-dependent propagation support and integrates
evidence from multiple reachable structures. Its closest competitors therefore
belong to the progressive and adaptive GNN line reviewed next.

\subsection{Query-Conditioned Progressive and Adaptive Propagation}
\label{subsec:gnn_for_kg}

Relation-aware GNNs provide a general mechanism for encoding multi-relational
neighborhoods. R-GCN uses relation-specific transformations, and CompGCN jointly
composes entity and relation representations during message passing
\citep{schlichtkrull2018modeling,vashishth2019composition}. GraIL extracts an
enclosing subgraph for each entity pair and performs relation-aware aggregation
over that fixed subgraph \citep{teru2020grail}. Neighborhood-aware models also
combine graph self-attention with pre-training to enhance local structural
representations \citep{wu2023neighborhood}. These architectures demonstrate the
value of relational neighborhoods, but their forward computation is conducted
on a predefined full graph or an independently constructed pair-specific
subgraph rather than on a support that evolves with the reasoning depth.

Progressive GNN reasoners align neural propagation with the increasing length of
relational paths. NBFNet generalizes the Bellman--Ford algorithm by learning the
operators used to aggregate path representations, whereas RED-GNN recursively
constructs query-centered relational digraphs and propagates over newly reached
entities \citep{zhu2021neural,zhang2022knowledge}. DiffusionE further formulates
knowledge propagation as an anisotropic diffusion process to regulate semantic
message flow \citep{cao2024diffusione}. By exposing layer-wise reachable
structures, these methods make multi-path aggregation more explicit than
triple-level scoring. Nevertheless, exhaustive expansion may admit many weakly
relevant entities and edges as the propagation radius increases.

Adaptive methods address this issue by controlling the propagation support.
DPMPN learns an input-dependent subgraph through attentive message flow,
AdaProp incrementally samples involved entities from a query-aware distribution,
and A*Net prioritizes promising nodes and edges to balance reasoning coverage
and computation \citep{xu2019dpmpn,zhang2022adaprop,zhu2023astarnet}. TAGNet
instead restricts message passing to a distance-based window, reducing empty and
redundant propagation without repeatedly activating the entire reachable region
\citep{shomer2023distance}, while multi-layer Top-$p$ message passing dynamically
retains high-probability entities to control propagation cost
\citep{liu2025topp}. These studies establish that query-dependent support
selection is central to efficient KG reasoning. For the closest adaptive
selectors, however, routing is primarily determined by scalar attention,
sampling probabilities, or priority scores; structurally constrained methods
impose a separate propagation schedule. The selected messages are then consumed
by a conventional hidden-state update.

STM-GNN is distinguished not by adaptive propagation alone. It constructs a
vector-valued transported evidence variable through relation--query-conditioned
restriction maps. The same variable determines which edges and entities enter
the next frontier and drives the subsequent hidden--momentum transition. This
shared evidence interface couples support evolution with state evolution rather
than treating routing and representation updating as independently
parameterized operations.

\subsection{Sheaf-Based Relational Transport}\label{subsec:sheaf_related}

Cellular sheaves enrich a graph by assigning vector spaces to cells and linear
restriction maps to their incidences, thereby representing how information must
be transformed before it can be compared across adjacent locations. Neural
Sheaf Diffusion learns such maps and diffusion operators to improve information
flow on heterophilic graphs \citep{bodnar2022neural}. Connection-based sheaf
networks study structured restriction maps and their associated Laplacians,
providing expressive alternatives to scalar edge weights while controlling the
cost of general matrix-valued maps \citep{barbero2022sheaf}. Both lines perform
diffusion on a fixed input graph and primarily target graph representation
learning rather than query-conditioned multi-hop reasoning.

Knowledge Sheaves connects the sheaf formalism directly to KGs by interpreting
embedding models as approximate global sections constrained by schema-induced
local consistency \citep{gebhart2023knowledge}. Its objective is to characterize
and learn globally compatible KG embeddings. In contrast, STM-GNN constructs
relation--query-conditioned diagonal restrictions on a dynamically expanded
local graph for each reasoning query. The resulting transported edge evidence
is not only an intermediate compatibility signal: it also controls structural
routing and depth-wise state evolution. Thus, the sheaf component serves as a
query-specific transport mechanism inside progressive inference rather than as
a static embedding constraint or a fixed-graph diffusion operator.

\subsection{Depth-Wise State Dynamics on Graphs}
\label{subsec:state_dynamics_related}

Recurrent units preserve information through learned hidden-state transitions,
and gated graph neural networks apply this principle to iterative message
passing \citep{cho2014learning,li2016gated}. Such updates describe a first-order
depth recurrence in which the next representation depends on the current state
and aggregated messages. Momentum ResNets introduce an explicit momentum
variable and interpret the resulting update as a second-order dynamical system
\citep{sander2021momentum}. GraphCON similarly derives graph-coupled oscillator
dynamics for deep graph learning \citep{rusch2022graphcon}. These dynamical
models improve information preservation across depth, but their state variables
are defined over a fixed set of graph nodes.

Progressive KG reasoners face a different setting: the computational domain
changes as new entities enter the active frontier. Existing progressive and
adaptive models accommodate this structural growth but typically update entity
representations with first-order recurrent or feed-forward transitions
\citep{zhang2022knowledge,zhang2022adaprop}. STM-GNN bridges these two settings
by coupling a second-order hidden--momentum evolution with an evolving support.
Historical hidden and momentum states are zero-extended to newly activated
entities, and the retained transported evidence supplies the input to the next
state transition. This construction makes support growth and memory evolution
parts of the same depth-wise reasoning process.

\section{Preliminaries and Problem Formulation}\label{sec:preliminaries}

This section separates the reasoning task from its STM-GNN realization. We
first define the observable graph and evaluation setting, then formalize the
evolving propagation support, introduce the sheaf transport primitive, and
finally state the shared-evidence transition to be learned.

\subsection{Task Definition and Evaluation Setting}
\label{subsec:problem_definition}

Let
$\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{obs}}=(\mathcal{V},\mathcal{R},
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}})$ denote the graph observable to a reasoner, where
$\mathcal{V}$ and $\mathcal{R}$ are the entity and relation sets and
$\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}}\subseteq
\mathcal{V}\times\mathcal{R}\times\mathcal{V}$ is the set of observable
facts. We consider transductive link prediction, in which the entity and
relation vocabularies are shared across training, validation, and test queries.
Given a tail-prediction query $q=(s,r_q,?)$, the model computes a score
$F_\theta(v\mid q,\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{obs}})$ for every
$v\in\mathcal{V}$. Let $\mathcal{E}^{+}$ denote the set of known positive
facts. The correct answer set of $q$ is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{Y}_{q}
=
\{v\in\mathcal{V}\mid(s,r_q,v)\in\mathcal{E}^{+}\}.
\end{equation}

The learning objective is to rank members of $\mathcal{Y}_{q}$ ahead of all
incorrect entities. Head prediction is converted to tail prediction through an
inverse query $(o,r_q^{-1},?)$. We adopt the standard filtered protocol
\citep{bordes2013transe}: when one correct answer is ranked, the other known
answers in $\mathcal{Y}_{q}$ are removed from its competitor set. Importantly,
the model scores the full entity set rather than a preselected candidate list.

\subsection{Augmented Graph and Progressive Support}
\label{subsec:augmented_graph}

Progressive reasoning expands a query-dependent support over observable facts.
To permit propagation in either direction and retain historical entities across
adjacent depths, we introduce
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{R}^{-1}=\{r^{-1}\mid r\in\mathcal{R}\},
\qquad
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}
=
\{(v,r^{-1},u)\mid(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}}\}.
\end{equation}
The augmented relation and edge sets are
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathcal{R}}
=
\mathcal{R}\cup\mathcal{R}^{-1}\cup\{r_{\mathrm{id}}\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{obs}}
=
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}}\cup\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{obs}}^{-1}
\cup\{(v,r_{\mathrm{id}},v):v\in\mathcal{V}\},
\end{equation}
where $r_{\mathrm{id}}$ is the identity relation. We write the resulting graph
as
$\widetilde{\mathcal{G}}_{\mathrm{obs}}
=(\mathcal{V},\widetilde{\mathcal{R}},
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{obs}})$. Identity edges provide a structural
channel through which an already active entity can remain represented at the
next depth.

Let $\mathcal{A}_{q}^{(l)}$ denote the active entity set after depth $l$ for
query $q$. We omit the query subscript below for readability. Starting from
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}^{(0)}=\{s\},
\end{equation}
one-hop expansion at depth $l$ produces
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}
=
\{(u,r,v)\in\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}_{\mathrm{obs}}
\mid u\in\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)}\},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{C}^{(l)}
=
\{v\mid (u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}\},
\end{equation}
and the newly reached entities are
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{N}^{(l)}
=
\mathcal{C}^{(l)}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)}.
\end{equation}
Following progressive and adaptive GNN reasoners
\citep{zhu2021neural,zhang2022knowledge,zhang2022adaprop}, the next support must
satisfy
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)}
\subseteq
\mathcal{A}^{(l)}
\subseteq
\mathcal{C}^{(l)},
\qquad
\left|\mathcal{A}^{(l)}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)}\right|
\leq k,
\label{eq:support_constraints}
\end{equation}
where $k$ is the maximum number of newly admitted entities per query and depth.
Thus, historical entities retain their depth trajectories, whereas newly
reached entities compete for a bounded propagation budget.

\subsection{Sheaf-Based Relational Transport}\label{subsec:sheaf_preliminaries}

A cellular sheaf assigns a vector space, called a stalk, to each graph cell and
a linear restriction map to every incidence between cells
\citep{hansen2019spectral,bodnar2022neural}. For a relational edge
$e=(u,r,v)$, let $\mathcal{F}(u)$, $\mathcal{F}(v)$, and $\mathcal{F}(e)$
denote the stalks associated with its endpoints and the edge, and let
$R_{u\rightarrow e}:\mathcal{F}(u)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(e)$ and
$R_{v\rightarrow e}:\mathcal{F}(v)\rightarrow\mathcal{F}(e)$ be the endpoint
restriction maps. In general, these maps need not be square or invertible.
When the three stalks have the same dimension and $R_{v\rightarrow e}$ is
invertible, however, an endpoint-to-endpoint transport can be induced as
\begin{equation}
T_{u\rightarrow v\mid e}
=
R_{v\rightarrow e}^{-1}R_{u\rightarrow e}.
\label{eq:generic_sheaf_transport}
\end{equation}
This operator first maps a source vector into the common edge stalk and then
expresses it in the target coordinates. It therefore provides a principled
primitive for transforming relational evidence before aggregation. STM-GNN
instantiates this transport with query-conditioned, depth-specific diagonal
restriction maps, yielding a scalable coordinate-wise operator for relational
evidence alignment on the evolving support.

\subsection{Coupled Evolving-Support Reasoning}\label{subsec:coupled_problem}

Progressive reasoning must make two interdependent decisions at every depth:
which newly reached entities are sufficiently relevant to enter the bounded
support, and how the entity states should evolve on that new domain. Let
$X^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{|\mathcal{A}^{(l)}|\times d}$ denote a generic
query-conditioned state with
$\operatorname{dom}(X^{(l)})=\mathcal{A}^{(l)}$, and let
$\mathcal{Z}^{(l)}=\{z_e^{(l)}:e\in
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}\}$ be the edge-level evidence produced at
depth $l$. We seek a transition of the form
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{Z}^{(l)}
&=\Psi_q^{(l)}
\left(X^{(l-1)},\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}\right),\\
\mathcal{A}^{(l)}
&=\mathfrak{R}_q^{(l)}
\left(\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)},\mathcal{N}^{(l)},
\mathcal{Z}^{(l)};k\right),\\
X^{(l)}
&=\mathfrak{U}_q^{(l)}
\left(X^{(l-1)},\mathcal{A}^{(l)},\mathcal{Z}^{(l)}\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:coupled_transition_problem}
\end{equation}
subject to Eq.~\eqref{eq:support_constraints}. Here, $\Psi_q^{(l)}$ constructs
edge evidence, $\mathfrak{R}_q^{(l)}$ evolves the support, and
$\mathfrak{U}_q^{(l)}$ updates the states. The coupling requirement is that both
support evolution and state evolution are functions of the same
$\mathcal{Z}^{(l)}$, rather than outputs of independently parameterized routing
and updating representations. Learning these transitions together with an
all-entity readout defines the coupled evolving-support reasoning problem. The
next section instantiates $\mathcal{Z}^{(l)}$ by query-conditioned sheaf
transport and instantiates $X^{(l)}$ by hidden--momentum states.

\section{STM-GNN: Query-Conditioned Evidence Dynamics}\label{sec:proposed_model}

\subsection{Framework Overview}\label{subsec:stm_overview}

STM-GNN instantiates the shared-evidence transition in
Eq.~\eqref{eq:coupled_transition_problem} with
$\mathcal{Z}^{(l)}=\mathcal{M}^{(l)}$ and
$X^{(l)}=(H^{(l)},M^{(l)})$. Each STM transition begins with deterministic
one-hop expansion and subsequently applies three learned operators:
query-conditioned sheaf transport, evidence-induced support evolution, and
momentum-coupled state evolution. The term \emph{coupled} is essential. The
transport operator produces edge evidence $\mu_e^{(l)}$; preview aggregation of
the same evidence determines the next active set $\mathcal{A}^{(l)}$; and the
retained evidence drives the new hidden and momentum states. The updated hidden
state then conditions transport at the next layer, thereby closing the
transition.

More formally, after obtaining $\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}$ and
$\mathcal{N}^{(l)}$, STM-GNN computes an edge-evidence collection
$\mathcal{M}^{(l)}=\{\mu_e^{(l)}\}$ and applies
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\mathcal{M}^{(l)}
&=\mathfrak{S}_{q}^{(l)}
\left(H^{(l-1)},\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}\right),\\
\mathcal{A}^{(l)}
&=\mathfrak{R}_{q}^{(l)}
\left(\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)},\mathcal{N}^{(l)},
\mathcal{M}^{(l)};k\right),\\
\left(H^{(l)},M^{(l)}\right)
&=\mathfrak{D}_{q}^{(l)}
\left(H^{(l-1)},M^{(l-1)},
\mathcal{A}^{(l)},\mathcal{M}^{(l)}\right),
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:stm_decomposition}
\end{equation}
where $\mathfrak{S}_{q}^{(l)}$, $\mathfrak{R}_{q}^{(l)}$, and
$\mathfrak{D}_{q}^{(l)}$ denote the sheaf transport, support evolution, and
depth evolution operators, respectively. Their composition constitutes one STM
transition, rather than three independently attached modules.

After $L$ transitions, STM-GNN concatenates each entity's hidden and momentum
states and produces all-entity link-prediction scores. Fig.~\ref{fig:overall_framework}
summarizes this closed process; the following subsections define its operators
in execution order.

\subsection{Query-Conditioned Sheaf Transport}\label{subsec:sheaf_module}

The first learned operator determines how relational evidence changes before it
is compared and aggregated at a target entity. For each expanded edge
$e=(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}$, we first compose the source
state and layer-specific relation embedding:
\begin{equation}
x_e^{(l)}=h_u^{(l-1)}+e_r^{(l)}.
\end{equation}
We then generate positive endpoint scales for each incidence role
$\xi\in\{u,v\}$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
s_{e,\xi}^{(l)}
=\operatorname{softplus}\bigl(&
a_{\xi,\mathrm{rel}}^{(l)}\odot e_r^{(l)}
+a_{\xi,\mathrm{qry}}^{(l)}\odot e_{r_q}^{(l)}\\
&+a_{\xi,\mathrm{int}}^{(l)}\odot
\left(e_r^{(l)}\odot e_{r_q}^{(l)}\right)
+b_{\xi}^{(l)}\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:sheaf_scales}
\end{equation}
Here,
$a_{\xi,\mathrm{rel}}^{(l)},a_{\xi,\mathrm{qry}}^{(l)},
a_{\xi,\mathrm{int}}^{(l)},b_{\xi}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$ are
layer-specific parameters shared across edges. Relation specificity is supplied
by $e_r^{(l)}$, query specificity by $e_{r_q}^{(l)}$, and their element-wise
interaction permits the same edge relation to induce different transports under
different queries.
These scales instantiate query-conditioned diagonal restriction maps
\begin{equation}
R_{u\rightarrow e}^{(l)}=\operatorname{Diag}(s_{e,u}^{(l)}),
\qquad
R_{v\rightarrow e}^{(l)}=\operatorname{Diag}(s_{e,v}^{(l)}).
\label{eq:restriction_maps}
\end{equation}
The source-to-target transport operator is consequently
\begin{equation}
T_e^{(l)}
=
\left(R_{v\rightarrow e}^{(l)}+\varepsilon I\right)^{-1}
R_{u\rightarrow e}^{(l)},
\label{eq:sheaf_transport}
\end{equation}
where $\varepsilon>0$ ensures numerical stability. Because the restrictions are
diagonal, its action can be computed without constructing dense matrices:
\begin{equation}
T_e^{(l)}x_e^{(l)}
=
\frac{s_{e,u}^{(l)}}{s_{e,v}^{(l)}+\varepsilon}
\odot x_e^{(l)}.
\end{equation}

Transport and edge relevance play different roles. $T_e^{(l)}$ determines how
individual feature coordinates are transformed, whereas a scalar attention
coefficient determines how strongly the whole edge contributes:
\begin{equation}
\alpha_e^{(l)}
=
\sigma\!\left(
w_{\alpha}^{(l)\top}
\rho\!\left(
W_h^{(l)}h_u^{(l-1)}
+W_r^{(l)}e_r^{(l)}
+W_q^{(l)}e_{r_q}^{(l)}
\right)
\right),
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\mu_e^{(l)}
=
\alpha_e^{(l)}T_e^{(l)}x_e^{(l)}.
\label{eq:transported_evidence}
\end{equation}
In the implementation, $\rho$ is the ReLU activation, the projection matrices
map from the hidden dimension $d$ to an attention dimension $a$, and
$w_{\alpha}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{a}$. Thus, $T_e^{(l)}$ provides feature-wise
coordinate transport, while $\alpha_e^{(l)}$ controls edge-level relevance.
The resulting $\mu_e^{(l)}$ is the common evidence variable used by both
downstream operators. Bias terms in the attention and routing projections are
omitted from the equations for readability.

\subsection{Evidence-Induced Support Evolution}\label{subsec:topk_module}

The second learned operator uses the transported evidence to determine where the
next transition will take place. Unlike routing with an independently learned
node representation, support evolution here is derived directly from the
evidence in Eq.~\eqref{eq:transported_evidence}. We first preview the incoming
evidence at every reached entity:
\begin{equation}
p_v^{(l)}
=
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}\\t(e)=v}}
\mu_e^{(l)}.
\end{equation}
The query-conditioned routing score is
\begin{equation}
g_v^{(l)}
=
w_g^{(l)\top}
\tanh\!\left(W_p^{(l)}p_v^{(l)}+W_{q,g}^{(l)}e_{r_q}^{(l)}\right).
\end{equation}
Here, $W_p^{(l)},W_{q,g}^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}$ and
$w_g^{(l)}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$.

Historical entities remain active through their identity edges, while newly
reached entities compete independently for each query. Let
$k_l=\min(k,|\mathcal{N}^{(l)}|)$ denote the effective routing budget at depth
$l$. The hard support is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{A}^{(l)}
=
\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)}
\cup
\operatorname{TopK}\!\left(\mathcal{N}^{(l)},g^{(l)},k_l\right).
\label{eq:active_support}
\end{equation}
The corresponding retained edge set is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(l)}
=
\{e\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}
\mid t(e)\in\mathcal{A}^{(l)}\}.
\label{eq:retained_edges}
\end{equation}
When $|\mathcal{N}^{(l)}|\leq k$, all newly reached entities are retained. When
$|\mathcal{N}^{(l)}|>k$, we make the evidence-induced selection trainable with
a budget-aware straight-through estimator. Let
$b_v^{(l)}=\mathbb{I}[v\in\mathcal{A}^{(l)}]$ be the hard routing decision and
define a temperature-controlled soft allocation over newly reached entities:
\begin{equation}
\pi_v^{(l)}
=
k_l
\frac{\exp(g_v^{(l)}/\tau)}
{\sum_{u\in\mathcal{N}^{(l)}}\exp(g_u^{(l)}/\tau)},
\qquad v\in\mathcal{N}^{(l)},
\label{eq:routing_relaxation}
\end{equation}
where $\tau>0$ is the routing temperature. The straight-through gate is
\begin{equation}
\omega_v^{(l)}
=
\operatorname{sg}\!\left(b_v^{(l)}-\pi_v^{(l)}\right)+\pi_v^{(l)},
\label{eq:routing_gate}
\end{equation}
where $\operatorname{sg}(\cdot)$ stops the gradient
\citep{bengio2013estimating}. Historical entities retain unit gates. The hard
support governs next-depth expansion, while the straight-through relaxation
enables end-to-end optimization through the corresponding soft allocation.

\subsection{Momentum-Coupled State Evolution}\label{subsec:momentum_module}

The third learned operator preserves depth-wise evidence history while the state
domain changes. When $\mathcal{A}^{(l)}$ introduces new entities, we first
extend historical states to the current active set:
\begin{equation}
\bar h_v^{(l-1)}
=
\begin{cases}
h_v^{(l-1)}, & v\in\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)},\\
\mathbf{0}, & v\in\mathcal{A}^{(l)}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)},
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\bar m_v^{(l-1)}
=
\begin{cases}
m_v^{(l-1)}, & v\in\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)},\\
\mathbf{0}, & v\in\mathcal{A}^{(l)}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{(l-1)}.
\end{cases}
\end{equation}
The retained transported evidence then produces the layer-wise driving signal
\begin{equation}
f_v^{(l)}
=
\phi\!\left(
W_f^{(l)}
\sum_{\substack{e\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}\\t(e)=v}}
\omega_{t(e)}^{(l)}\mu_e^{(l)}
\right).
\label{eq:driving_signal}
\end{equation}

STM-GNN then evolves a depth-wise momentum state and a hidden state jointly:
\begin{equation}
m_v^{(l)}
=
\gamma\bar m_v^{(l-1)}+(1-\gamma)f_v^{(l)},
\label{eq:momentum_update}
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
h_v^{(l)}
=
\bar h_v^{(l-1)}+\beta m_v^{(l)},
\label{eq:hidden_update}
\end{equation}
where $\gamma\in[0,1)$ controls depth-wise evidence persistence and $\beta>0$
controls its injection into the hidden state. For an entity active at consecutive
depths, Eqs.~\eqref{eq:momentum_update}--\eqref{eq:hidden_update} imply
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
h_v^{(l)}
=\;&h_v^{(l-1)}
+\gamma\left(h_v^{(l-1)}-h_v^{(l-2)}\right)\\
&+\beta(1-\gamma)f_v^{(l)}.
\end{aligned}
\label{eq:second_order_depth}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:second_order_depth} exposes the second-order depth structure:
the next state depends on the previous displacement as well as the current
driving signal. The momentum state is therefore an internal component of the
STM transition, rather than a recurrent layer applied after graph propagation.

\paragraph{Interpretation and limiting cases.}
The STM transition contains several standard propagation schemes as limiting
cases. First, if the source and target restrictions coincide and
$\varepsilon\rightarrow0$, then $T_e^{(l)}\rightarrow I$, and
$\mu_e^{(l)}$ reduces to a conventional relation-aware attentive message.
Second, if $k\geq|\mathcal{N}^{(l)}|$, all newly reached entities are retained,
so evidence-induced support evolution becomes unpruned progressive propagation.
Third, setting $\gamma=0$ removes depth-wise momentum persistence and yields the
first-order residual update
$h_v^{(l)}=\bar h_v^{(l-1)}+\beta f_v^{(l)}$.
These limiting cases clarify that evidence transformation, support control, and
depth dynamics are coordinated degrees of freedom within a single STM
transition.

\subsection{Prediction and Transition Regularization}\label{subsec:objective}

After $L$ transitions, states are zero-extended from $\mathcal{A}^{(L)}$ to the
full entity set:
\begin{equation}
\widehat h_v^{(L)}
=
\begin{cases}
h_v^{(L)}, & v\in\mathcal{A}^{(L)},\\
\mathbf{0}, & v\notin\mathcal{A}^{(L)},
\end{cases}
\qquad
\widehat m_v^{(L)}
=
\begin{cases}
m_v^{(L)}, & v\in\mathcal{A}^{(L)},\\
\mathbf{0}, & v\notin\mathcal{A}^{(L)}.
\end{cases}
\label{eq:full_entity_extension}
\end{equation}
The final readout and entity score are
\begin{equation}
z_v^{(L)}
=
[\widehat h_v^{(L)}\|\widehat m_v^{(L)}],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{score}(v\mid q)=w_o^\top z_v^{(L)},
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}
P(v\mid q)
=
\frac{\exp(\operatorname{score}(v\mid q))}
{\sum_{u\in\mathcal{V}}\exp(\operatorname{score}(u\mid q))}.
\label{eq:all_entity_probability}
\end{equation}
Training queries are instantiated per positive triple. For a mini-batch
$\mathcal{B}$ of query--answer pairs $(q,o)$, the all-entity prediction loss is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}
=
-\frac{1}{|\mathcal{B}|}
\sum_{(q,o)\in\mathcal{B}}
\log P(o\mid q).
\end{equation}
Other known answers to the same query are handled by the filtered protocol at
evaluation time, as defined in Section~\ref{subsec:problem_definition}.

The STM transition is further constrained at the edge and depth levels.
Transport consistency compares the source evidence and the target driving signal
after both are restricted to the edge stalk:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tr}}
=
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(l)}|}
\sum_{e=(u,r,v)\in\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{ret}}^{(l)}}
\alpha_e^{(l)}
\left\|
R_{u\rightarrow e}^{(l)}x_e^{(l)}
-R_{v\rightarrow e}^{(l)}f_v^{(l)}
\right\|_2^2.
\label{eq:transport_consistency}
\end{equation}
Depth continuity penalizes abrupt changes in the momentum trajectory on the
evolving support:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{depth}}
=
\frac{1}{L}\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\frac{1}{|\mathcal{A}^{(l)}|}
\sum_{v\in\mathcal{A}^{(l)}}
\left\|m_v^{(l)}-\bar m_v^{(l-1)}\right\|_2^2.
\label{eq:depth_continuity}
\end{equation}
The overall objective is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}
=
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{pred}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{tr}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tr}}
+\lambda_{\mathrm{depth}}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{depth}}.
\label{eq:overall_objective}
\end{equation}
Rather than introducing unrelated auxiliary tasks,
$\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{depth}}$ regularize the same
evidence transition across an edge and across depth, respectively.

\subsection{Computational Characterization}\label{subsec:computational_characterization}

Let $B$ be the batch size, $d$ the hidden dimension, $a$ the attention
dimension, $E_l=|\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{exp}}^{(l)}|$,
$C_l=|\mathcal{C}^{(l)}|$, and $A_l=|\mathcal{A}^{(l)}|$, where the set sizes
are summed over the queries in a batch. At depth $l$, diagonal restriction
generation and transport require $\mathcal{O}(E_ld)$ operations, attention
projections require $\mathcal{O}(E_lda)$, candidate and state projections
require $\mathcal{O}(C_ld^2)$, and partial top-$k$ selection requires
$\mathcal{O}(C_l\log k)$. Constructing and normalizing the final all-entity
scores additionally costs $\mathcal{O}(B|\mathcal{V}|d)$. The overall time
complexity is therefore
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{O}\!\left(
\sum_{l=1}^{L}
\left(E_lda+C_ld^2+C_l\log k\right)
+B|\mathcal{V}|d
\right),
\end{equation}
where the two uses of transported evidence---previewing candidates and updating
states---affect only the constant factor of the edge computation. The peak
activation memory is
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{O}\!\left(
\max_{1\leq l\leq L}
\left(E_ld+C_ld+A_ld\right)
+B|\mathcal{V}|
\right).
\end{equation}
The resulting computation scales with the explored edges and candidates at each
depth, while diagonal transport avoids storing edge-specific dense $d\times d$
transformation matrices.

\section{Experiments and Analysis}\label{sec:experiments}

\subsection{Research Questions}\label{subsec:research_questions}

The evaluation is organized around four research questions. \textbf{RQ1} asks
whether STM-GNN improves all-entity link prediction across KGs with different
structural characteristics. \textbf{RQ2} examines whether coupling transport,
frontier evolution, and momentum recurrence is more effective than optimizing
the corresponding operations independently. \textbf{RQ3} analyzes whether the
learned transition produces query-relevant transport, compact propagation
supports, and continuous depth trajectories. \textbf{RQ4} studies the trade-off
among predictive performance, routing budget, and computational cost.

\subsection{Experimental Settings}\label{subsec:experimental_settings}

\subsubsection{Datasets}\label{subsec:datasets}

We evaluate STM-GNN on five transductive KG reasoning benchmarks: Family,
UMLS, WN18RR, FB15k-237, and NELL-995. Family contains kinship relations and
provides a compact setting with regular compositional patterns. UMLS is a dense
biomedical KG whose relatively small entity set is connected by diverse semantic
relations. WN18RR is a lexical benchmark derived from WordNet with inverse
relation leakage removed. FB15k-237 contains heterogeneous real-world relations
from Freebase, whereas NELL-995 provides a substantially larger and sparser
entity space. Together, the five datasets cover small regular graphs, dense
domain graphs, lexical hierarchies, heterogeneous relations, and large sparse
graphs. We use the transductive splits and preprocessing protocol associated
with RED-GNN \citep{zhang2022knowledge}. Dataset statistics are summarized in

\subsubsection{Baselines and Evaluation Metrics}\label{subsec:baselines_metrics}

Following the comparison setting shown in
Tables~\ref{tab:main_results_small} and~\ref{tab:main_results_large}, we
consider three groups of baselines: triple-based models, path-based models, and
GNN-based models. The triple-based baselines include ConvE, RotatE, and QuatE;
the path-based baselines include MINERVA, Neural LP, DRUM, and RNNLogic; and
the graph-based baselines include pLogicNet, CompGCN, DPMPN, NBFNet, RED-GNN,
AdaProp, A*Net, TAGNet, and DiffusionE. The latter group is particularly
important because it includes full-graph propagation, progressive propagation,
learned routing, and efficiency-oriented path selection. These methods cover
representative paradigms from local triple scoring, differentiable rule or path
reasoning, and query-guided graph propagation
\citep{dettmers2018convolutional,sun2019rotate,yang2017neurallp,
sadeghian2019drum,qu2020rnnlogic,qu2019probabilistic,zhang2019quaternion,
das2018minerva,
vashishth2019composition,xu2019dpmpn,zhu2021neural,zhang2022knowledge,
zhang2022adaprop,zhu2023astarnet,shomer2023distance,cao2024diffusione}.

We compare all methods under the transductive setting. A baseline result is used
only when its dataset split, filtered evaluation protocol, and prediction
direction match the present setting. The final tables will distinguish values
reproduced with official implementations from values reported by the original
papers; unavailable or unfinished results are denoted by ``--''.

Following common practice in KG completion, we use Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR),
Hits@1, and Hits@10 as the main evaluation metrics. MRR measures the average
reciprocal rank of the correct answer, while Hits@k measures the proportion of
queries for which the correct entity appears in the top-$k$ predictions. Higher
values of these metrics indicate better reasoning performance.

\subsubsection{Implementation Details}\label{subsec:implementation}

The model is trained with a validation-driven tuning protocol. Hyperparameters
such as hidden dimension, reasoning depth, retained frontier size, routing
temperature, momentum coefficient $\gamma$, residual coefficient $\beta$, and
loss weights $\lambda_{\mathrm{tr}}$ and $\lambda_{\mathrm{depth}}$ are selected
according to validation MRR. For each dataset, the fact and training pools are
repartitioned using the dataset-specific fact ratio, following the progressive
reasoning protocol used by RED-GNN. We use Adam optimization and early stopping,
save the checkpoint with the highest filtered validation MRR, and evaluate the
test set only after model selection. Final STM-GNN results will be reported as
the mean and standard deviation over five independent random seeds. All methods
are evaluated using filtered ranking in both prediction directions. Dropout is
applied to the layer-wise driving signal, with the activation function and
dropout rate selected according to validation MRR.

\subsection{Transition Behavior Analysis}\label{subsec:transition_analysis}

To answer RQ3, we characterize STM transitions through target coverage, support
compactness, transport selectivity, and depth continuity. For a set of
query--answer pairs $\mathcal{Q}$, target coverage and the average support ratio
at depth $l$ are defined as
\begin{equation}
\operatorname{Cov}^{(l)}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{(q,o)\in\mathcal{Q}}
\mathbb{I}[o\in\mathcal{A}_{q}^{(l)}],
\qquad
\operatorname{SR}^{(l)}
=\frac{1}{|\mathcal{Q}|}\sum_{(q,o)\in\mathcal{Q}}
\frac{|\mathcal{A}_{q}^{(l)}|}
{\max\{1,|\mathcal{C}_{q}^{(l)}|\}}.
\end{equation}
Edge-level analysis compares relation--query transport scales and transport
consistency on retained and discarded candidates. Support-level analysis reports
coverage, active nodes, retained edges, and propagated messages over depth.
Depth-level analysis compares momentum displacement, adjacent-layer state
variation, and prediction quality for the complete and first-order variants.
Together, these diagnostics test whether compact support and smoother depth
dynamics coincide with preserved answer evidence and improved prediction.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}

This paper addresses progressive multi-hop knowledge graph reasoning by
formulating the reasoning process as query-conditioned evidence dynamics on an
evolving local graph. STM-GNN realizes this formulation through a coupled STM
transition: query-conditioned sheaf restrictions transform relation-aware edge
evidence, the transported evidence induces the active propagation support, and
the retained evidence drives a momentum-coupled state update. The updated state
then conditions the transition at the next reasoning depth. In this way,
evidence transformation, routing, and depth-wise accumulation are coordinated
through a shared transition rather than modeled as unrelated stages.
